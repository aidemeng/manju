# Story Development

Use this reference for `outline` mode. Its purpose is to turn a premise into an 80-episode visual narrative without forcing one formula onto every genre.

## Establish the story engine

Before plotting episodes, make these decisions explicit:

- **Audience promise:** the repeatable feeling viewers return for, such as discovery, justice, romance, dread, competence, or catharsis.
- **Protagonist drive:** a concrete external goal plus the internal belief that complicates it.
- **Story mechanism:** the ability, institution, mystery, relationship, or rule that repeatedly creates choices.
- **Cost and limit:** what the mechanism cannot solve, what using it risks, and how opponents can respond.
- **Visual identity:** two or three recurring materials, colors, locations, or effects that distinguish the work.
- **Opposition ladder:** increasingly capable opponents whose goals make sense from their own perspective.
- **Ending claim:** what the finale proves or disproves about the protagonist's starting belief.

Do not invent a system, romance, revenge plot, or power fantasy unless it serves the user's premise.

## Default five-act map

| Act | Episodes | Narrative job | Required change |
|---|---:|---|---|
| I — Trigger | 1–16 | Begin in motion, reveal the engine, deliver the first meaningful result | The protagonist chooses the story and cannot fully return |
| II — Expansion | 17–32 | Broaden the world, alliances, opposition, and cost | The original method succeeds but creates a larger problem |
| III — Reversal | 33–48 | Reinterpret a core fact and shift strategy | A midpoint discovery changes who or what the conflict is about |
| IV — Breakdown | 49–64 | Make the old method fail, collect consequences, and rebuild | The protagonist loses a key advantage and makes a harder choice |
| V — Finale | 65–80 | Converge relationships and plot, pay off setup, show aftermath | The central conflict resolves and the ending claim becomes visible |

If the user changes the episode count, preserve the proportional dramatic jobs instead of keeping these exact boundaries.

## Episode contract

Each episode should contain:

1. A visible opening change, question, or unstable situation.
2. One episode-level objective.
3. Resistance that forces a meaningful choice.
4. A payoff, reversal, or new understanding—not only setup.
5. A final image or decision that creates a specific next question.

A hook is not always a threat. Rotate among revelation, dilemma, promise, emotional turn, discovery, reversal, deadline, victory with a cost, and consequence. Avoid using the same hook family or conflict resolution in adjacent episodes.

## Visual progression

Plan escalation through changed meaning, not only larger explosions:

- Revisit a location after its power relationship changes.
- Reuse a prop after its owner, function, or emotional meaning changes.
- Let costume, posture, lighting, and blocking show relationship movement.
- Reserve the most expensive crowd, destruction, transformation, or environment shots for structural peaks.
- Follow a spectacle episode with a cheaper but consequential character or mystery episode when appropriate.

## Continuity ledgers

Maintain three compact ledgers in the outline:

- **Setup/payoff:** setup episode, visible clue, intended payoff range, actual payoff.
- **Mechanism limits:** rule, evidence, known exceptions, consequence of violation.
- **Relationship state:** pair, current alignment, unresolved pressure, next irreversible change.

Do not plant a clue without a plausible payoff window. If a later idea changes the truth, update the ledger and affected episodes rather than relying on memory.

## Outline self-check

- Episode 1 contains action and a concrete question, not a lore lecture.
- Every act changes the protagonist's strategy or status.
- Every run of five episodes includes at least one payoff.
- No major character disappears for long stretches without explanation.
- Escalation changes choices, relationships, or understanding—not only power level.
- The final act resolves the central promise and the major planted clues.
- Romantic or sexual material follows the adult and consent constraints in `SKILL.md`.
