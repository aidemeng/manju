# Quality Review

Use this reference for `review` mode after a batch or when the user requests an audit. Review evidence first; do not rewrite merely to match personal taste.

## Inputs

Read `project.md`, `outline.md`, `characters.md`, `catalog.md`, the target episode files, the previous episode, and any prior report that affects the batch. If an input is missing, list the resulting blind spot.

Use these hook-family labels consistently: revelation, dilemma, promise, emotional turn, discovery, reversal, deadline, costly victory, and consequence. Classify by dramatic function, not surface wording.

## Scorecard

Score each dimension from 0 to 10 and cite episode and shot evidence.

| Dimension | 10-point standard |
|---|---|
| Story advance | Every episode changes the story and fulfills its catalog advance |
| Character consistency and agency | Choices follow established motives while major characters remain active |
| Causality and timeline | Actions, knowledge, travel, injuries, and consequences connect plausibly |
| Story mechanism | Abilities or rules obey their limits and create decisions rather than shortcuts |
| Episode pacing | Openings start in motion, middles escalate, and payoffs receive enough space |
| Visual generatability | Shots are concrete, stable, spatially clear, and not overloaded |
| Hook quality and diversity | Endings open specific questions without repeating adjacent hook functions |
| Continuity and payoff | Setups, props, relationships, and prior consequences carry forward |
| Production feasibility | Costly shots are intentional and the batch remains producible |
| Safety and platform fit | Adult status, consent, violence, claims, and supplied platform rules are respected |

Use this scale consistently: `9–10` strong, `7–8` workable with limited issues, `4–6` material weakness, `0–3` broken or absent.

## Overall result

- `90–100`: pass.
- `80–89`: pass with optional improvements.
- `60–79`: revise and re-review before continuing.
- Below `60`: stop and repair structural problems.
- A severe safety problem requires revision regardless of total score.

Do not inflate the score to avoid revisions. Do not reduce it for genre preferences that contradict the user's approved brief.

State the gate rationale separately from the number. If a severe safety issue overrides the numeric band, record that override explicitly.

## Finding contract

Every finding must include:

1. Severity: blocking, required, or optional.
2. Evidence: file, episode, and shot or catalog row.
3. Broken expectation: the upstream fact or scorecard criterion.
4. Consequence: what viewers, continuity, or production loses.
5. Smallest effective revision.

Separate diagnosis from the rewritten passage. When revision is requested, preserve unaffected beats and edit the smallest useful scope.

## Batch statistics

Report:

- Episode count and missing files.
- Hook families in sequence.
- Payoffs delivered and setups still open.
- High-visual-weight episodes and any unusually costly shots identified in production notes.
- New characters, locations, abilities, or resources not present upstream.
- Dialogue-heavy shots and overloaded shots.

## Re-review

On re-review, check the prior blocking and required findings first. Mark each resolved, partially resolved, or unresolved with fresh evidence. Recalculate all dimensions affected by the edits; do not blindly preserve the previous score.
